Promises :

A Promise is an object representing the eventual completion or failure of an asynchronous operation.
 Since most people are consumers of already-created promises, this guide will explain consumption of returned promises before explaining how to create them.

Promise Object Properties
A JavaScript Promise object can be:

Pending
Fulfilled
Rejected
The Promise object supports two properties: state and result.

While a Promise object is "pending" (working), the result is undefined.
When a Promise object is "fulfilled", the result is a value.
When a Promise object is "rejected", the result is an error object.

Green Computing
Definition:
Green computing refers to the practice of designing, using, and disposing of computers and IT resources in an environmentally friendly way. It aims to reduce energy consumption, minimize electronic waste, and lower the environmental impact of IT systems.

How Green Computing Works:
Energy-Efficient Hardware

Use devices that consume less power, such as energy-efficient processors and solid-state drives (SSDs).
Implement power-saving modes for idle systems.
Virtualization

Run multiple systems on a single machine using virtualization to reduce the number of physical devices.
Cloud Computing

Shift data and processing tasks to energy-efficient cloud servers.
Recycling and Proper Disposal

Reuse or recycle old hardware to reduce e-waste.
Use of Renewable Energy

Power data centers and IT facilities with solar, wind, or other renewable energy sources.
Paperless Workflows

Digitize documents and processes to reduce paper usage.
Advantages of Green Computing:
Environmental Benefits

Reduces carbon emissions and conserves natural resources.
Cost Savings

Lower energy bills due to efficient hardware and energy-saving practices.
Improved Efficiency

Virtualization and cloud computing maximize resource utilization.
Positive Brand Image

Companies adopting green computing can attract environmentally conscious customers.
Compliance with Regulations

Helps businesses adhere to environmental laws and standards.
Disadvantages of Green Computing:
High Initial Costs

Purchasing energy-efficient hardware and renewable energy systems can be expensive.
Performance Trade-Offs

Some energy-saving devices may have lower performance compared to traditional ones.
Recycling Challenges

Proper disposal and recycling of e-waste require specialized processes and facilities.
Dependency on New Technology

Transitioning to green computing often requires new skills and tools.
Implementation Complexity

Implementing green IT practices can be complex for large organizations.
Summary
Green computing is a sustainable way of using IT resources while protecting the environment. While it offers significant long-term benefits, initial costs and implementation challenges can be barriers. Organizations and individuals adopting green computing contribute to a cleaner, greener planet.
